\subsection{The PALETTE-Aug transform}
\label{subsec:m-transform}

Let $x$ be a training volume min-max normalized to $[0,1]$, $x_v$ its
intensity at voxel $v$, and $F = \{v : x_v > 0.01\}$ its foreground.
PALETTE-Aug applies four steps at every training iteration, shown in order in
\cref{fig:method-pipeline}(b)--(e).

\paragraph{1. Intensity partition.} We run 1-D $k$-means on the foreground
intensities $\{x_v : v \in F\}$ with $k$ sampled per iteration from
$\{2,\dots,6\}$, producing $k$ intensity classes $C_1, \dots, C_k$
(\cref{fig:method-pipeline}b). This partition groups voxels by intensity alone, with no label map, so it
applies unchanged to any modality or contrast. Occasionally it is skipped and
step 3 applies one global remap to the whole foreground; all step
probabilities are in \cref{sec:suppl-config}.

\paragraph{2. Spatial sub-parcellation.} A Voronoi partition seeded with points sampled within each
class $C_i$ splits it into sub-regions $R_{i,1}, \dots, R_{i,m_i}$ that are
spatially localized and similar in intensity (\cref{fig:method-pipeline}c).
Two voxels of equal intensity in different parts of the anatomy can thus fall
in different sub-regions and receive independent contrast changes. Each class is
usually, but not always, sub-split this way (\cref{sec:suppl-config}).

\paragraph{3. Signed affine remap.} For each sub-region $R$, let $\bar{x}_R$
denote its mean intensity, $\mu_R \sim U(0,1)$ a resampled target mean, and
draw $\alpha_R \sim \pm U(0.5, 2.0)$ (a magnitude in $[0.5, 2.0]$ with an
independently sampled sign). Every voxel $v \in R$ is remapped as
\begin{equation}
  y_v = \mu_R + \alpha_R \, (x_v - \bar{x}_R).
  \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
Because \cref{eq:affine} is a per-region affine function of the
\emph{real} intensity $x_v$, it preserves the relative structure of the
signal within each region: edges, gradients, and partial-volume transitions
between adjacent voxels are scaled, not replaced. Only the contrast between regions is randomized, and $\alpha_R < 0$ also
inverts local contrast (\cref{fig:method-pipeline}d). Background voxels are zeroed at this step (and
remain zero through the rest of the pipeline). Half the time, the result is then Gaussian-blurred
(\cref{sec:suppl-config}).

\paragraph{4. Anatomical-label affine remap (optional).} An intensity partition
does not always separate the regions of interest: where a target's boundary is
already faint, steps 1--3 can place both sides in one region, or give them
similar contrasts, and weaken it further. Step 4 therefore remaps each
anatomical label $\ell$ on its own: we draw a new target mean $\mu_\ell \sim
U(0,1)$ and signed scale $\alpha_\ell \sim \pm U(0.5, 2.0)$, as in step 3, and
apply \cref{eq:affine} \emph{again} to $\ell$'s voxels of the step-3 output
(\cref{fig:method-pipeline}e), so the label remap composes with the
unsupervised one and keeps its internal structure. Since labels are independent of the unsupervised partition, the label remap
restores a full-strength contrast step at every true label border, whether or
not step 3 kept it; the anatomy itself is unchanged. It uses only the task's own labels and is optional:
without it, PALETTE-Aug reduces to steps 1--3, e.g.\ on unlabeled data. An
optional second blur and a foreground z-score normalization then give the
training input.


\subsection{Training protocol}
\label{subsec:m-training}

PALETTE-Aug is applied on-the-fly as one contrast-augmentation operator inside
an existing GPU-accelerated nnU-Net augmentation
pipeline~\cite{molinier2026auglab,isensee2021nnunet}, alongside that
pipeline's standard spatial and artifact augmentations; it adds no learned
parameters and runs on the GPU inside that pipeline. One configuration
($k$-range, sub-region count, blur schedule, $\alpha$ range;
\cref{sec:suppl-config}) is set once and reused unchanged for every dataset
and modality: PALETTE-Aug's hyperparameters are never tuned to a task.

\subsection{Causal ablation: isolating texture preservation}
\label{subsec:m-ablation}

PALETTE-Aug differs from SynthSeg in two ways at once: the partition (unsupervised
$k$-means and Voronoi vs.\ an anatomical label map) and the fill (remapped real
intensities vs.\ Gaussian noise). A direct comparison cannot separate them.

We construct a controlled variant, \emph{noise-fill}, that keeps PALETTE-Aug's
partition (steps 1 and 2) and draws the same target mean $\mu_R$ for each
sub-region, but replaces what fills it: instead of remapping the
sub-region's real intensities with \cref{eq:affine}, it fills the sub-region
with SynthSeg-style Gaussian noise, $\mu_R + \sigma_R
\epsilon$ with $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ i.i.d.\ per voxel and $\sigma_R$
drawn uniformly from $[0.05, 0.25]$ per sub-region; the label step (step 4) is
noise-filled the same way. The partition and the target means are
PALETTE-Aug's own, so a difference between the two isolates whether the real
intensity structure inside each region is kept or replaced by noise
(\cref{fig:fill-swap-example}).

We report
this comparison separately for \emph{interface-bounded} targets (a normal
structure bounded by another tissue class) and \emph{appearance-defined}
targets (a pathology identified by abnormal signal within a tissue), assigned
by anatomy; the texture hypothesis predicts a larger gain on the latter.
