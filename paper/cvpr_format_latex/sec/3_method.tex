\subsection{The PALETTE-Aug transform}
\label{subsec:m-transform}

Let $x$ be a training volume min-max normalized to $[0,1]$, $x_v$ its
intensity at voxel $v$, and $F = \{v : x_v > 0.01\}$ its foreground.
PALETTE-Aug changes contrast region by region with one operation, a signed
affine remap. We define it first, then build the regions it acts on in four
steps, in the order in which the ablation adds them
(\S\ref{subsec:m-ablation}, \cref{fig:method-pipeline}b--e).

\paragraph{Signed affine remap.} For a region $R$, let $\bar{x}_R$ denote its
mean intensity, $\mu_R \sim U(0,1)$ a resampled target mean, and draw
$\alpha_R \sim \pm U(0.5, 2.0)$ (a magnitude in $[0.5, 2.0]$ with an
independently sampled sign). Every voxel $v \in R$ is remapped as
\begin{equation}
  y_v = \mu_R + \alpha_R \, (x_v - \bar{x}_R).
  \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
Because \cref{eq:affine} is affine in the \emph{real} intensity $x_v$, it
preserves the relative structure of the signal within the region: edges,
gradients, and partial-volume transitions between adjacent voxels are scaled,
not replaced. Only the contrast between regions is randomized, and
$\alpha_R < 0$ also inverts local contrast.

\paragraph{1. Intensity partition.} We run 1-D $k$-means on the foreground
intensities $\{x_v : v \in F\}$ with $k$ sampled per iteration from
$\{2,\dots,6\}$, producing $k$ intensity classes $C_1, \dots, C_k$, each
with its own target contrast (\cref{fig:method-pipeline}b). This partition
groups voxels by intensity alone, with no label map, so it applies unchanged
to any modality or contrast. Occasionally it is skipped and the whole
foreground is remapped as one region; all step probabilities are in
\cref{sec:suppl-config}.

\paragraph{2. Anatomical-label remap (optional).} An intensity partition
does not always separate the regions of interest: where a target's boundary is
already faint, the partition can place both sides in one region, or give them
similar contrasts, and weaken it further. Each anatomical label $\ell$ is
therefore remapped on its own: we draw a new target mean $\mu_\ell \sim
U(0,1)$ and signed scale $\alpha_\ell \sim \pm U(0.5, 2.0)$ and apply
\cref{eq:affine} \emph{again} to $\ell$'s voxels after the region remap
(\cref{fig:method-pipeline}c), so the label remap composes with the
unsupervised one and keeps its internal structure. Since labels are
independent of the partition, this restores a full-strength contrast step at
every true label border; the anatomy itself is unchanged. It uses only the
task's own labels and is optional, \eg on unlabeled data.

\paragraph{3. Spatial sub-parcellation.} A Voronoi partition seeded with
points sampled within each class $C_i$ splits it into sub-regions
$R_{i,1}, \dots, R_{i,m_i}$ that are spatially localized and similar in
intensity, each with its own target contrast (\cref{fig:method-pipeline}d).
Two voxels of equal intensity in different parts of the anatomy can thus fall
in different sub-regions and receive independent contrast changes. Each class
is usually, but not always, sub-split this way (\cref{sec:suppl-config}).

\paragraph{4. Real fill.} Every sub-region, and then every label, is filled
through \cref{eq:affine} from its own real intensities, which keeps the
texture inside each region (\cref{fig:method-pipeline}e); the ablation
replaces exactly this fill with noise (\S\ref{subsec:m-ablation}). Background
voxels are zeroed and stay zero. The result is Gaussian-blurred half the time,
before and optionally after the label remap, and a foreground z-score
normalization gives the training input (\cref{sec:suppl-config}).


\subsection{PALETTE alone and PALETTE-Aug}
\label{subsec:m-training}

We use the transform in two configurations, both applied on the fly on the GPU
to half of the training iterations, with no learned parameters.
\emph{PALETTE alone} is the transform with spatial augmentation only
(rotation, scaling, flips); it is also the real-fill rung of the ablation
(\S\ref{subsec:m-ablation}). \emph{PALETTE-Aug}, our full method, adds the
transform as one operator to Auglab~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, a GPU nnU-Net
augmentation pipeline~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} of intensity, spatial and
artifact transforms and our strongest competitor (\cref{subsec:e-main}), so
PALETTE-Aug and Auglab differ by exactly this transform.

\subsection{Causal ablation: isolating texture preservation}
\label{subsec:m-ablation}

PALETTE-Aug differs from SynthSeg in two ways at once: the partition (unsupervised
$k$-means and Voronoi vs.\ an anatomical label map) and the fill (remapped real
intensities vs.\ Gaussian noise). A direct comparison cannot separate them.

We construct a controlled variant, \emph{noise-fill}, that keeps steps 1--3
of PALETTE-Aug (the same partition, label step and target mean $\mu_R$ for
each region) and changes only step 4: instead of remapping each region's real
intensities with \cref{eq:affine}, it fills the region with SynthSeg-style
Gaussian noise, $\mu_R + \sigma_R \epsilon$ with $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$
i.i.d.\ per voxel and $\sigma_R$ drawn uniformly from $[0.05, 0.25]$ per
region. Labels are refilled the same way, in their own Voronoi cells, so both
arms have sub-regions inside labels. The partition and the target means are
PALETTE-Aug's own, so a
difference between the two isolates whether the real intensity structure
inside each region is kept or replaced by noise
(\cref{fig:fill-swap-example}).

We report
this comparison separately for \emph{interface-bounded} targets (a normal
structure bounded by another tissue class) and \emph{appearance-defined}
targets (a pathology identified by abnormal signal within a tissue), assigned
by anatomy; the texture hypothesis predicts a larger gain on the latter.
