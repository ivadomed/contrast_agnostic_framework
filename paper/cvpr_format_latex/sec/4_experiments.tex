\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:e-setup}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Each model trains on one real source modality and is
tested on every modality of the same task, the protocol Auglab uses for
spine~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, here over eight tasks and with external
cohorts wherever one exists. No target-domain data is seen in training. All
methods share one nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} configuration and schedule
and differ only in input augmentation; the epoch budget is fixed per dataset
(\cref{sec:suppl-config}). Our full method adds the PALETTE-Aug transform as
one operator to the Auglab pipeline~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, so it and Auglab
differ by exactly that transform. Each method is trained on three
train/validation folds, and all three models are scored on the same held-out
test set. Significance tests are paired, use the patient (for ON-Harmony, the session)
as the unit, and are
Holm-corrected~\cite{holm1979} (\cref{sec:suppl-persetting}).

\paragraph{Datasets.} We evaluate on eight tasks drawn from sixteen datasets
(full roster in \cref{tab:datasets}): abdominal organs, healthy brain, brain tumor, MS
lesions, mandible, breast lesions, spine and pelvic musculoskeletal anatomy,
across MRI, CT and dental CBCT. Brain tumor and MS lesions use
BraTS2024-GLI~\cite{deverdier2024brats2024} and
Open-MS~\cite{lesjak2018openms}. Each (task, training modality) pair is one \emph{setting}, eighteen in all,
tested on every contrast of its task, the training contrast included;
\emph{out-of-domain} (OOD) results exclude the training contrast, also in
external cohorts. External cohorts are pooled into their task as extra held-out test columns,
not reported as separate tasks. CHAOS~\cite{kavur2021chaos} transfers to the
CT of AMOS~\cite{ji2022amos} and SLIVER07~\cite{heimann2009comparison}.
Mandible (ToothFairy2~\cite{bolelli2025toothfairy2}, CBCT only) transfers to
the CT and MR of HaN-Seg~\cite{podobnik2023hanseg} and the CT of
PDDCA~\cite{raudaschl2017pddca}, so every held-out contrast but its own CBCT
comes from another dataset. Breast (I-SPY2~\cite{li2022ispy2}) transfers to
Duke-Breast-MRI~\cite{saha2018duke,garrucho2025mamamia},
I-SPY1~\cite{hylton2012ispy1} and ACRIN-6698~\cite{partridge2018acrin6698},
whose diffusion-weighted MRI resembles neither training modality. Spine (a
CT/Dixon cohort) transfers to the T1w and T2w of
SPIDER~\cite{vandergraaf2024spider}. Pelvis is built from the TotalSegmentator CT and MRI
releases~\cite{wasserthal2023totalseg,dantonoli2025totalsegmri}, two unpaired
cohorts. ON-Harmony~\cite{warrington2025onharmony} reference labels are
silver-standard FreeSurfer~\cite{fischl2012freesurfer} parcellations of the
T1w, registered to the other contrasts. External volumes that image more of the body
than the training cohort are cropped to its field of view before prediction,
identically for every method (\cref{sec:suppl-data}).

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare against five methods. (1) \emph{Baseline},
nnU-Net with its default augmentation. (2)
\emph{SynthSeg-noEM}~\cite{billot2023synthseg}, which synthesizes each image
from the label map alone and ignores the acquired scan; it receives each
task's training labels, which form a dense parcellation only on ON-Harmony.
(3) \emph{SynthSeg-EM}, our re-implementation of SynthSeg's EM
extension~\cite[\S5.4]{billot2023synthseg}, which first subdivides every
label by expectation-maximization clustering of the image. (4)
\emph{SRCSM}~\cite{thaler2025semrandconv}, a semantic random-convolution
augmentation outside both the label-generative and the Auglab families. (5)
\emph{Auglab}~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, a published GPU augmentation
pipeline. It is a competitor in its own right, not an ablation of ours; since
our method adds one operator to it, comparing the two also tests that operator.

\paragraph{PALETTE-Aug is the best method overall.} PALETTE-Aug has the best
Dice on six of the eight tasks. Across tasks it is significantly better than every
competitor ($p<10^{-10}$ each, \cref{tab:meta}). Within single tasks it
significantly outperforms every competitor on five tasks (BraTS-GLI, CHAOS,
Open-MS, Breast, Spine), three of five on Mandible and Pelvis, and two
of five on ON-Harmony. The cross-task test generalizes to new patients on these eight tasks, not to
new tasks. Counting each task as one unit instead, PALETTE-Aug beats Auglab on seven of
eight tasks (one-sided sign test $p=0.035$) and SRCSM on five of the
seven tasks where SRCSM was run ($p=0.23$).

Given sparse labels, SynthSeg-noEM paints one Gaussian background around the
labeled structures and falls below the baseline on six of the eight tasks,
every one with sparse labels; SynthSeg-EM, which subdivides the labels by
clustering the image, closes most of that gap.

\paragraph{No competitor is reliably second.} The best competitor alternates
between Auglab (BraTS-GLI, Open-MS, Breast, Spine) and SRCSM (ON-Harmony,
Mandible, Pelvis), the two tie on CHAOS, and SRCSM fails on Breast ($16.4$ Dice).
Picking the right one would require knowing the answer in advance.
PALETTE-Aug is first or second on every task but one.

\paragraph{Where PALETTE-Aug does not win.} The one-sided test above cannot show
that a competitor is ahead, so on ON-Harmony and Mandible we also ran the reverse test on
the same patient-level design (Holm-corrected across competitors within the
task). On ON-Harmony SRCSM leads by $0.1$ Dice and neither method is
significantly ahead. On Mandible SRCSM ($+1.3$ Dice, $p=6\times10^{-4}$) and
Auglab ($+0.6$, $p=2.5\times10^{-3}$) are significantly ahead, and PALETTE-Aug
is third. In the Mandible ablation (\S\ref{subsec:e-ablation}), real fill
helps significantly; Dice drops only once Auglab is added
(\cref{tab:ladder-full}).

\paragraph{Boundary distance.} The 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95)
is reported in the supplement
(\cref{tab:meta-hd95}). PALETTE-Aug has the lowest HD95 on six of the eight
tasks (BraTS-GLI, CHAOS, ON-Harmony, Open-MS, Spine, Pelvis) and is significantly
better than every competitor across tasks. It ties Auglab on Breast and is significantly behind SRCSM,
Auglab and the baseline on Mandible.

\subsection{Causal ablation: when does texture matter?}
\label{subsec:e-ablation}

Does keeping the real texture help beyond PALETTE-Aug's partition, and on
which targets? We test this with a ladder in which each rung adds one ingredient:
baseline $\rightarrow$ $+k$-means $\rightarrow$ $+$label remap $\rightarrow$
$+$Voronoi $\rightarrow$ real-intensity fill $\rightarrow$ $+$Auglab. The three
partition rungs fill every region with SynthSeg-style Gaussian noise. All rungs
share one pipeline (\cref{sec:method}) and differ only in which steps are on;
before $+$Auglab, the only other augmentation is spatial (with flips). This
step from $+$Voronoi to real fill, the \emph{fill swap}, changes only the fill:
partition, sub-region count, target-mean distribution and both blur passes are
unchanged. Any change at this step therefore comes from keeping the real
within-region intensity structure instead of noise.

In our data every large relative gain from real fill is on an
appearance-defined target, but not every appearance-defined target shows one
(\cref{fig:ladder,tab:dissociation}). We report each gain relative to its
noise-fill Dice, because the same few points are a small share of a high
starting Dice and a large share of a low one (\cref{sec:discussion} notes the
alternative); the three largest gains are the same relative or absolute.
They are BraTS-GLI T1c ($+43.6\%$, $+13.3$ Dice) and T1n ($+18.1\%$, $+7.2$),
both significant after correction over the fourteen rows, and Open-MS FLAIR
($+39.3\%$, $+7.7$), which improves for all eight test patients but is not
significant after that correction. No interface-bounded row gains more than
$6.2\%$; the largest, CHAOS T2spir, adds $5.1$ points from a noise-fill Dice of
$82.0$. Pooled per task (\cref{fig:ladder}), the step is worth $+14.8\%$ on
BraTS-GLI and $+15.8\%$ on Open-MS, against $+3.9\%$ on CHAOS, $+2.0\%$ on
Mandible and $-3.0\%$ on ON-Harmony. Not every appearance-defined setting
gains: Open-MS T1w, BraTS-GLI T2w and FLAIR, and both Breast rows ($-2.8\%$
and $+1.3\%$, four cohorts pooled by contrast) show no Dice gain.

ON-Harmony labels 31 brain regions in the FreeSurfer label
set~\cite{fischl2012freesurfer}: ventricles, cortex, white matter,
cerebellum, brainstem and eight deep grey nuclei. Nearly all are bounded by
another tissue class (CSF against tissue, grey against white matter), so we
group it with CHAOS as interface-bounded. At the task level it is the only
task where the swap significantly hurts, with sessions as units. It is also
the only task labeling nearly the whole brain; with one such task, we cannot
tell whether that is why.

\paragraph{Does the fill swap change texture?}
The ladder shows that the fill swap changes Dice, not that it changes texture.
We check this without any segmentation, with the normalized-gradient-field
(NGF) similarity~\cite{haber2006ngf} between each augmented volume and its
source scan. NGF measures how well image gradients align: it is high when
edges and fine structure are kept and falls to about $1/3$ for isotropic
noise. \Cref{fig:ngf-dose} follows NGF and Dice along the same rungs on Open-MS
FLAIR. The three noise-fill rungs stay at NGF $0.45$--$0.46$: above the noise floor,
since partition edges survive, but far below the source. Their Dice still rises
from $11.2$ to $19.6$, through contrast randomization, not texture. Only the real-fill swap raises both, NGF to $0.81$ and Dice to
$27.3$. On the same scale, PALETTE-Aug without Auglab (the real-fill rung) keeps
significantly more source texture than Auglab (paired Wilcoxon, $30$ scans;
FLAIR $p=3.2\times10^{-3}$, T1w $p=1.2\times10^{-6}$), and the SynthSeg
variants stay far below the source (\cref{tab:ngf}).
