\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{subsec:e-setup}

\paragraph{Tasks and data.} \Cref{tab:tasks} lists the eight tasks, their
cohorts, the modalities each is trained on and the contrasts it is tested on,
across MRI, CT and dental CBCT. Every (task, training modality) pair is one
\emph{setting}, eighteen in all; each setting trains its own models, which are
tested on every contrast of the task, the training contrast included.
\emph{Out-of-domain} (OOD) results exclude every scan of the training
contrast, whichever cohort it comes from. External cohorts add held-out test
contrasts to their task; they are not separate tasks.

\paragraph{Training and evaluation.} Each model trains on one real source
modality and is tested on every modality of its task, the protocol Auglab uses
for spine~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, here over eight tasks. Splits are by
subject: every scan of a test subject, in any modality, is held out of
training, and no target-domain data is seen in training. All methods share
the same 3D nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} configuration, optimizer,
learning-rate schedule and per-task epoch budget (\cref{sec:suppl-config}),
and differ only in input augmentation. Each method trains three models on
three train/validation folds, all scored on the same held-out test set.
PALETTE's configuration is set once and never tuned to a task
(\cref{sec:suppl-config}). Significance tests are paired over patients and
Holm-corrected~\cite{holm1979} (\cref{sec:suppl-stats}).

\paragraph{Compared methods.} We compare against five methods
(\cref{tab:methods}), each run as its authors propose, with its own
augmentation recipe on every training iteration: \emph{nnU-Net} with its
default augmentation; SynthSeg~\cite{billot2023synthseg} as released
(\emph{SynthSeg-noEM}); its EM label-subdivision variant
(\emph{SynthSeg-EM}), developed for cardiac MRI~\cite[\S5.4]{billot2023synthseg}
and re-implemented here since its code was not released;
SRCSM~\cite{thaler2025semrandconv}; and Auglab~\cite{molinier2026auglab} in
its default configuration (\cref{tab:suppl-aug-probs}). The SynthSeg variants
receive each task's training labels. SynthSeg-noEM synthesizes from the label
map alone and was designed for dense labels, which only Brain provides.
SynthSeg and SRCSM run without the Auglab pipeline, as published
(\cref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{PALETTE-Aug is the best method overall.} PALETTE-Aug has the best
Dice on seven of the eight tasks (\cref{tab:meta}). We test its advantage at
two levels. Within each individual task (bottom row), it significantly
outperforms every competitor on five tasks (Glioma, MS, Breast, Spine,
Pelvis), all but SRCSM on Abdomen ($85.6$ vs.\ $85.9$), and three of five on
Brain and Mandible. Pooling the test patients of all eight tasks, each task
weighted equally, it is significantly better than every competitor:
$p=1.1\times10^{-13}$ against Auglab, the closest, and $p\le10^{-35}$ against
the others. With patients as the unit, this pooled test generalizes to new
patients of these eight tasks, not to new tasks; with the task as the unit,
PALETTE-Aug beats Auglab on seven of eight tasks (one-sided sign test
$p=0.035$) and SRCSM on six of the seven tasks where SRCSM was run
($p=0.063$).

SynthSeg-noEM falls below nnU-Net on the six sparse-label tasks; on Brain,
the one dense-label task, it is within $2.6$ Dice of PALETTE-Aug.

\paragraph{No competitor is reliably second.} The best competitor depends on
the task: Auglab on Glioma, MS, Breast, Spine and Pelvis, SRCSM on Abdomen,
Brain and Mandible. SRCSM is also the weakest method on Breast ($14.7$ Dice).
Picking the right competitor would require knowing the answer in advance;
PALETTE-Aug is first on every task but Mandible.

\paragraph{Where PALETTE-Aug does not win.} On Mandible, SRCSM is first
($79.8$), Auglab second ($78.3$) and PALETTE-Aug third ($77.7$). The one-sided
test above cannot show that a competitor is ahead, so we also ran the reverse
test (same design, Holm-corrected across competitors within the task): only
Auglab is significantly ahead of PALETTE-Aug ($+0.6$ Dice,
$p=3\times10^{-3}$); SRCSM's $+2.1$ is not significant ($p=0.15$).
Interestingly, PALETTE alone ties Auglab ($78.3$).

\paragraph{PALETTE without Auglab.} PALETTE alone
(\cref{subsec:m-training}) is as strong as the full method: over the seven
tasks with both, it is significantly better than every competitor, Auglab
included (Dice $p=4\times10^{-5}$), and does not differ significantly from
PALETTE-Aug (two-sided $p=0.60$). PALETTE-Aug's margin over the competitors
therefore does not come from the Auglab pipeline. Per task, adding Auglab
raises Dice on Glioma and Breast, lowers it on MS (significantly) and slightly
on Abdomen, Brain and Mandible, and leaves Pelvis unchanged (\cref{tab:meta}).
PALETTE thus composes with a full augmentation pipeline without losing its
advantage, but which operators help alongside it depends on the task, and
choosing them per task may add more; we have not identified which Auglab
operator is responsible for the losses.

\paragraph{Boundary distance.} On HD95 (\cref{tab:meta-hd95}), PALETTE-Aug is
lowest on six of the eight tasks and significantly better than every
competitor with all tasks pooled; it ties Auglab on Breast and trails Auglab
and nnU-Net on Mandible.

\subsection{Causal ablation: when does texture matter?}
\label{subsec:e-ablation}

Does keeping the real texture help beyond PALETTE-Aug's partition, and on
which targets? We test this with a ladder:
nnU-Net $\rightarrow$ $+k$-means $\rightarrow$ $+$label remap $\rightarrow$
$+$Voronoi $\rightarrow$ real-intensity fill $\rightarrow$ $+$Auglab. The
first rung is nnU-Net with its default augmentation. The next four rungs share
one pipeline (\cref{sec:method}) whose only other augmentation is spatial
(with flips), so the first step also drops nnU-Net's intensity augmentation;
from there on, each rung adds one ingredient. The three partition rungs fill
every region with SynthSeg-style Gaussian noise. The
step from $+$Voronoi to real fill, the \emph{fill swap}, changes only the fill:
partition, sub-region count, target-mean distribution and both blur passes are
unchanged (inside labels, the noise arm draws its own Voronoi cells). Any change at this step therefore comes from keeping the real
within-region intensity structure instead of noise.

In our data every large gain from real fill is on an appearance-defined
target, but not every appearance-defined target shows one
(\cref{fig:ladder,tab:dissociation}). The swap is tested once per task,
pooling its training modalities (\cref{sec:suppl-stats}); the per-setting
values are a breakdown of that test. Pooled per task, the swap adds $+6.8$
Dice on MS (all eight test patients improve) and $+4.6$ on Glioma, against
$+3.5$ on Abdomen, $+3.1$ on Pelvis, $-0.7$ on Brain and $-1.6$ on Mandible; each of these tasks passes the correction except
Mandible, Brain with a loss. The four largest per-setting gains are Glioma T1c ($+12.7$
Dice), MS FLAIR ($+7.7$), Glioma T1n ($+6.0$) and MS T1w ($+5.9$); no
interface-bounded setting gains more than $+5.9$ (Pelvis CT). Breast gains
only $+1.5$, mostly from T2w training ($+2.7$; T1-CE training $+0.4$), and
Glioma T2w and FLAIR show no gain ($+1.1$, $-1.3$). Relative to the noise-fill
Dice (\cref{fig:ladder}) the ranking at the top is unchanged.

ON-Harmony labels 31 brain regions in the FreeSurfer label
set~\cite{fischl2012freesurfer}. Nearly all are bounded by
another tissue class (CSF against tissue, grey against white matter), so we
group it with Abdomen as interface-bounded. At the task level it is the only
task where the swap significantly hurts ($-0.7$ Dice), with sessions as units. It is also
the only task labeling nearly the whole brain; with one such task, we cannot
tell whether that is why.

\paragraph{Does the fill swap change texture?}
The ladder shows that the fill swap changes Dice, not that it changes texture.
We check this without any segmentation, with the normalized-gradient-field
(NGF) similarity~\cite{haber2006ngf} between each augmented volume and its
source scan. NGF measures how well image gradients align: it is $1$ when all
edges and fine structure are kept and about $1/3$ for isotropic noise.
\Cref{fig:ngf-methods} reports it for every method on the seven ablation tasks.
The noise fill keeps only the partition edges (mean NGF $0.44$); the real fill,
on the same partition, raises it to $0.83$, the highest of all methods.
PALETTE alone keeps more texture than every competitor on six tasks and ties
with Auglab on Brain (\cref{tab:ngf}). SynthSeg keeps little ($0.50$ with EM,
$0.37$ without, near the noise floor). PALETTE-Aug keeps less ($0.70$): it
applies PALETTE in half of its draws, on top of Auglab.
