\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:e-setup}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Each model trains on one real source modality and is
tested on every modality of the same task, the protocol Auglab uses for
spine~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, here over eight tasks and with external
cohorts wherever one exists. No target-domain data is seen in training. All
methods share one nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} configuration and schedule
and differ only in input augmentation; the epoch budget is fixed per dataset
(\cref{sec:suppl-config}). Our full method adds the PALETTE-Aug transform as
one operator to the Auglab pipeline~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, so it and Auglab
differ by exactly that transform. Each method is trained on three
train/validation folds, and all three models are scored on the same held-out
test set. Significance tests are paired, use the patient (for Brain, the session)
as the unit, and are
Holm-corrected~\cite{holm1979} (\cref{sec:suppl-stats}).

\paragraph{Datasets.} \Cref{tab:tasks} lists the eight tasks, the modalities
each is trained on and the contrasts it is tested on, across MRI, CT and dental
CBCT. Every (task, training modality) pair is one \emph{setting}, eighteen in
all; each setting trains its own models and is tested on every contrast of its
task, the training contrast included. \emph{Out-of-domain} (OOD) results exclude
the training contrast, also in external cohorts. The training cohorts are, in
table order, BraTS2024-GLI~\cite{deverdier2024brats2024},
Open-MS~\cite{lesjak2018openms}, I-SPY2~\cite{li2022ispy2},
CHAOS~\cite{kavur2021chaos}, ON-Harmony~\cite{warrington2025onharmony},
ToothFairy2~\cite{bolelli2025toothfairy2}, a CT/Dixon spine cohort and
TotalSegmentator~\cite{wasserthal2023totalseg,dantonoli2025totalsegmri}. The
external test cohorts are Duke~\cite{saha2018duke,garrucho2025mamamia},
I-SPY1~\cite{hylton2012ispy1} and ACRIN-6698~\cite{partridge2018acrin6698}
(Breast), AMOS~\cite{ji2022amos} and SLIVER07~\cite{heimann2009comparison}
(Abdomen), HaN-Seg~\cite{podobnik2023hanseg} and
PDDCA~\cite{raudaschl2017pddca} (Mandible), and
SPIDER~\cite{vandergraaf2024spider} (Spine); they are extra held-out test
columns of their task, not separate tasks (sources per contrast:
\cref{tab:datasets}). ON-Harmony's reference labels are silver-standard
FreeSurfer~\cite{fischl2012freesurfer} parcellations of the T1w, registered to
the other contrasts. External volumes that image more of the body than the
training cohort are cropped to its field of view before prediction, identically
for every method (\cref{sec:suppl-data}).

\paragraph{Baselines.} \Cref{tab:methods} compares the five competitors:
nnU-Net with its default augmentation (\emph{Baseline}),
SynthSeg~\cite{billot2023synthseg} as released, developed for the brain
(\emph{SynthSeg-noEM}), and its EM label-subdivision variant, developed for
cardiac MRI~\cite[\S5.4]{billot2023synthseg} and re-implemented here since its
code was not released (\emph{SynthSeg-EM}), both given each
task's training labels, which are a dense parcellation only on Brain;
SRCSM~\cite{thaler2025semrandconv}; and Auglab~\cite{molinier2026auglab}, a
competitor in its own right. Each competitor is run as its authors propose:
the SynthSeg variants and SRCSM synthesize on every training iteration with
their own augmentation recipe, and Auglab uses its default configuration
(\cref{tab:suppl-aug-probs}). SRCSM's published pipeline also
histogram-matches test images to the source domain; we compare training-time
augmentations under one predictor, so no method gets a test-time step.
On sparse task labels, SynthSeg-noEM (designed for dense labels) is a
reference point for synthesizing from labels alone, not a tuned competitor. Our method adds one
operator to Auglab, so comparing the two tests that operator; the other
competitors run without the Auglab pipeline (\cref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{PALETTE-Aug is the best method overall.} PALETTE-Aug has the best
Dice on six of the eight tasks. Across tasks it is significantly better than every
competitor (\cref{tab:meta}): $p=1.1\times10^{-13}$ against Auglab, the
closest, and $p\le10^{-35}$ against the others. Within single tasks it
significantly outperforms every competitor on five tasks (Glioma, MS,
Breast, Abdomen, Spine) and three of five on Brain, Mandible and Pelvis. The cross-task test generalizes to new patients on these eight tasks, not to
new tasks. Counting each task as one unit instead, PALETTE-Aug beats Auglab on seven of
eight tasks (one-sided sign test $p=0.035$) and SRCSM on five of the
seven tasks where SRCSM was run ($p=0.23$).

SynthSeg-noEM falls below the baseline on six of the eight tasks, all with
sparse labels; on Brain, the one dense-label task, it is within $2.6$ Dice of
PALETTE-Aug, and SynthSeg-EM closes most of the gap elsewhere.

\paragraph{No competitor is reliably second.} The best competitor alternates
between Auglab (Glioma, MS, Breast, Spine) and SRCSM (Brain,
Mandible, Pelvis), the two tie on Abdomen, and SRCSM fails on Breast ($16.4$ Dice).
Picking the right one would require knowing the answer in advance.
PALETTE-Aug is first or second on every task but one.

\paragraph{Where PALETTE-Aug does not win.} The one-sided test above cannot show
that a competitor is ahead, so on Brain and Mandible we also ran the reverse test on
the same patient-level design (Holm-corrected across competitors within the
task). On Brain SRCSM leads by $0.1$ Dice and is not significantly
ahead ($p=1.0$). On Mandible SRCSM ($+1.3$ Dice, $p=6\times10^{-4}$) and
Auglab ($+0.6$, $p=2.5\times10^{-3}$) are significantly ahead, and PALETTE-Aug
is third. In the Mandible ablation (\S\ref{subsec:e-ablation}), real fill
helps significantly; Dice drops only once Auglab is added
(\cref{tab:ladder-full}).

\paragraph{PALETTE without Auglab.} The transform alone (the ladder's
real-fill rung) is as strong as the full method: over the six tasks with both,
it is significantly better than every competitor, Auglab included (Dice
$p=7\times10^{-5}$), and does not differ from PALETTE-Aug (two-sided
$p=0.68$). Adding Auglab raises task Dice on Glioma and Breast, lowers it on
MS (significantly), Abdomen and Mandible and leaves Pelvis unchanged
(\cref{tab:meta}), so the
two interact differently per task; we have not identified which Auglab operator is
responsible. \TODO{Paul: keep PALETTE-Aug as the headline?}

\paragraph{Boundary distance.} The 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95)
is reported in the supplement
(\cref{tab:meta-hd95}). PALETTE-Aug has the lowest HD95 on six of the eight
tasks (Glioma, Abdomen, Brain, MS, Spine, Pelvis) and is significantly
better than every competitor across tasks. It ties Auglab on Breast and is significantly behind SRCSM,
Auglab and the baseline on Mandible.

\subsection{Causal ablation: when does texture matter?}
\label{subsec:e-ablation}

Does keeping the real texture help beyond PALETTE-Aug's partition, and on
which targets? We test this with a ladder:
baseline $\rightarrow$ $+k$-means $\rightarrow$ $+$label remap $\rightarrow$
$+$Voronoi $\rightarrow$ real-intensity fill $\rightarrow$ $+$Auglab. The
baseline is nnU-Net with its default augmentation. The next four rungs share
one pipeline (\cref{sec:method}) whose only other augmentation is spatial
(with flips), so the first step also drops nnU-Net's intensity augmentation;
from there on, each rung adds one ingredient. The three partition rungs fill
every region with SynthSeg-style Gaussian noise. The
step from $+$Voronoi to real fill, the \emph{fill swap}, changes only the fill:
partition, sub-region count, target-mean distribution and both blur passes are
unchanged. Any change at this step therefore comes from keeping the real
within-region intensity structure instead of noise.

In our data every large gain from real fill is on an appearance-defined
target, but not every appearance-defined target shows one
(\cref{fig:ladder,tab:dissociation}). The four largest gains are Glioma T1c
($+12.7$ Dice) and T1n ($+6.0$), both significant after correction over the
sixteen settings, and MS FLAIR ($+7.7$; all eight test patients improve) and
MS T1w ($+5.9$), which with eight test patients cannot pass that correction.
No interface-bounded setting gains more than $+4.8$ (Pelvis CT, not
significant). Pooled per task (\cref{fig:ladder}), the swap adds $+6.8$ Dice
on MS and $+4.6$ on Glioma, against $+3.5$ on Pelvis, $+2.6$ on Abdomen,
$+0.1$ on Mandible and \TODO{Brain retrain} on Brain. Breast gains only
$+0.5$, all from T2w training ($+2.1$; T1-CE training loses $1.2$, both
significant), and Glioma T2w and FLAIR show no significant gain. Relative to
the noise-fill Dice the top of the ranking is unchanged (MS $+25\%$, Glioma
$+13\%$, interface-bounded tasks at most $+7\%$), but that view favors tasks
with a low starting Dice (\cref{sec:discussion}).

ON-Harmony labels 31 brain regions in the FreeSurfer label
set~\cite{fischl2012freesurfer}: ventricles, cortex, white matter,
cerebellum, brainstem and eight deep grey nuclei. Nearly all are bounded by
another tissue class (CSF against tissue, grey against white matter), so we
group it with Abdomen as interface-bounded. At the task level it is the only
task where the swap significantly hurts \TODO{Brain retrain: check sign}, with sessions as units. It is also
the only task labeling nearly the whole brain; with one such task, we cannot
tell whether that is why.

\paragraph{Does the fill swap change texture?}
The ladder shows that the fill swap changes Dice, not that it changes texture.
We check this without any segmentation, with the normalized-gradient-field
(NGF) similarity~\cite{haber2006ngf} between each augmented volume and its
source scan. NGF measures how well image gradients align: it is $1$ when all
edges and fine structure are kept and about $1/3$ for isotropic noise.
\Cref{fig:ngf-methods} reports it for every method on the seven ablation tasks.
The noise fill keeps only the partition edges (mean NGF $0.44$); the real fill,
on the same partition, raises it to $0.83$, the highest of all methods.
PALETTE alone keeps more texture than every competitor on six tasks and ties
with Auglab on Brain (\cref{tab:ngf}). SynthSeg keeps little ($0.50$ with EM,
$0.37$ without, near the noise floor). PALETTE-Aug keeps less than PALETTE
alone ($0.70$): it applies PALETTE in half of its draws, on top of the Auglab
chain, which acts on every draw.
