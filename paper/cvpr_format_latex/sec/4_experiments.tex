\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:e-setup}

\paragraph{Protocol.} We train a single real-intensity source modality per
dataset and evaluate on held-out modalities of the \emph{same} segmentation
task, using one fixed nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} configuration across
all settings. No target-domain data, of any modality, is seen during
training.

\paragraph{Datasets.} Eight train$\to$test settings spanning abdominal
CT/MRI, brain MRI, and brain tumor MRI:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CHAOS}~\cite{kavur2021chaos} (abdominal organ segmentation):
    train on T1-in-phase or T2-SPIR MRI, test cross-modality on held-out CT
    from AMOS~\cite{ji2022amos} and SLIVER07~\cite{heimann2009comparison}.
  \item \textbf{OpenHarmony} (brain segmentation, cross-contrast): train on
    T1w or T2w, test on the complementary contrast.
  \item \textbf{OpenMS}~\cite{lesjak2018openms} (brain MS-lesion
    segmentation, cross-contrast): train on FLAIR or T1w, test on held-out
    contrasts of the same lesion-segmentation task.
  \item \textbf{BraTS}~\cite{deverdier2024brats2024} (brain tumor
    segmentation, cross-contrast): train on T1n or T2w.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Baselines.} (1) \emph{No-augmentation}, the nnU-Net default
training recipe; (2) \emph{SynthSeg}~\cite{billot2023synthseg}
(\emph{SynthSeg-noEM}), the original label-generative variant, which
requires a dense anatomical label map per training subject; (3)
\emph{SynthSeg-EM}, the label-free variant described in the original work
but never released publicly -- we re-implement it as a fair, equally
label-free comparison; and (4) \emph{\texttt{auglab\_default}}, the
underlying augmentation pipeline~\cite{molinier2026auglab} with every
transform \emph{except} PALETTE, i.e.\ our own method with the one
contribution removed, reported as an ablation rather than an external
baseline since it is not a prior published method.

\paragraph{Statistics.} We report per-case paired Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests (not per-fold), with Holm correction for multiple comparisons, so
that significance reflects consistency across individual test cases rather
than aggregate noise.

PALETTE beats the no-augmentation baseline and the original SynthSeg-noEM
by a large margin in every setting, confirming that a synthetic-contrast
augmentation is necessary at all under single-source cross-contrast
evaluation: the no-augmentation baseline collapses whenever train and test
contrast differ (e.g.\ 36.7 Dice on CHAOS T2spir$\to$CT, against 86.0 for
PALETTE). Against the stronger, label-free SynthSeg-EM baseline the margin
narrows but remains significant in seven of eight settings, with a single
tie on OpenMS T1w.

Against \texttt{auglab\_default} -- PALETTE with the contrast transform
itself removed -- margins are consistently thin, between $+0.4$ and $+1.6$
Dice, and \texttt{auglab\_default} is the strongest competitor in every
setting. We report this honestly rather than only the more flattering
comparison to external baselines: the aggregate Dice margin alone is not
the paper's central evidence, and \cref{subsec:e-ablation} shows where and
why the method's effect concentrates.

\subsection{Where the advantage concentrates}
\label{subsec:e-crush}

The aggregate margin over \texttt{auglab\_default} is thin, but it is not
uniform. Consistent with the texture-preservation hypothesis in
\cref{sec:method}, the gap widens specifically on fine, texture-defined
structures and on the most contrast-shifted test modalities, rather than on
whole-organ boundaries that survive any reasonable augmentation. We report
per-structure breakdowns for the settings where this effect is largest
alongside the aggregate numbers above, so that it is verifiable case-by-case
rather than asserted from the average.

\subsection{Causal ablation: noise-fill}
\label{subsec:e-ablation}

To test whether texture preservation -- rather than the shared
$k$-means/Voronoi partitioning scheme -- causes PALETTE's advantage, we
compare against \emph{noise-fill} (\cref{subsec:m-ablation}), which holds
the partition and per-region statistics fixed and only replaces the
real-intensity remap with label-style Gaussian noise. On OpenMS-FLAIR,
noise-fill is significantly \emph{worse} than PALETTE ($\Delta = -2.24$
Dice, Holm-corrected $p = 0.0125$, case-level record 7 wins / 17 losses):
removing texture preservation while holding everything else constant hurts
performance specifically on this texture-defined lesion-segmentation
setting, which is the outcome the texture-preservation hypothesis predicts
rather than a regression to be explained away. Structures whose boundaries
are defined by sharp contrast, not fine internal texture, do not show the
same degradation, isolating texture preservation as the causal mechanism
rather than an artifact of the partitioning scheme the two methods share.
