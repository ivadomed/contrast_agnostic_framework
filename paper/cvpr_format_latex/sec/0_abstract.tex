\begin{abstract}
Segmentation networks trained on one MRI contrast or imaging modality often
fail on another, and annotating data for every new domain is costly.
Synthesis-based domain randomization such as SynthSeg generates training
images from label maps alone, but it needs dense label maps and discards the
texture of the real image. We introduce PALETTE-Aug, a contrast augmentation
that remaps the real intensities of an unsupervised partition of the image, so
that the texture inside every region survives, and that needs only the task's
own labels. We then ask when keeping texture matters. In a controlled ablation
over sixteen training settings, we hold the partition fixed and replace noise
fill with the real-intensity remap. The four largest gains, $+5.9$ to $+12.7$
Dice points, are all on targets defined by tissue appearance (brain tumor and
multiple sclerosis lesions), against at most $+4.8$ on targets bounded by an
anatomical interface. The converse does not hold: several appearance-defined
settings, including breast lesions, gain little or nothing. On eight
segmentation tasks across MRI, CT and dental CBCT, each trained on one modality
and tested on the others, PALETTE-Aug, which adds our transform to the Auglab
augmentation pipeline, has the highest Dice on six tasks and, with all tasks
pooled, is significantly better than each of five competing methods.
\end{abstract}
