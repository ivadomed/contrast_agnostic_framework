\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Introduction.} Segmentation networks trained on one MRI contrast or
imaging modality often fail on another, and annotating data for every new
domain is costly. Synthesis-based domain randomization such as SynthSeg
generates training images from label maps alone, but it needs dense label maps
and discards the texture of the real image. We introduce PALETTE-Aug, a
texture-preserving alternative that needs only the task's own labels, and ask
when texture matters for generalization.
\textbf{Methods.} PALETTE-Aug remaps the real intensities of an unsupervised
partition of the image, so the texture inside every region survives. We
benchmark it against five methods on eight segmentation tasks across MRI, CT
and dental CBCT: five whose targets are bounded by an anatomical interface
(healthy brain, multi-organ, healthy spine, mandible, pelvis) and three defined
by tissue appearance (brain tumor, multiple sclerosis lesions, breast lesions).
Each method trains on one modality and is tested in-domain and out-of-domain.
An ablation over sixteen training settings holds the partition fixed and
replaces noise fill with the real-intensity remap.
\textbf{Results.} PALETTE-Aug wins the benchmark overall: it has the highest
Dice on six of the eight tasks and, with all tasks pooled, is significantly
better than every competitor. In the ablation, keeping texture raises Dice by
$+6.8$ points on multiple sclerosis lesions and $+4.6$ on brain tumor, both
appearance-defined, against at most $+3.5$ on interface-bounded tasks. Breast
lesions, also appearance-defined, gain only $+0.5$.
\textbf{Conclusion.} Texture preservation helps most on targets defined by
tissue appearance rather than an anatomical interface, though not on all of
them. PALETTE-Aug generalizes best overall across eight segmentation tasks.
\end{abstract}
