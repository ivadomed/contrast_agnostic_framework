\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Introduction.} Deep learning for medical image segmentation generalizes
poorly across imaging modalities, and annotating data for every new domain is
prohibitively costly. Synthesis-based augmentation methods such as SynthSeg
address this by generating training images from label maps alone, but they
require dense label maps and discard the source image's texture. We introduce
PALETTE-Aug, a texture-preserving alternative, and ask when texture matters
for generalization.
\textbf{Methods.} We benchmark PALETTE-Aug against five methods on eight
segmentation tasks: five whose targets are bounded by an anatomical interface
(healthy brain, multi-organ, healthy spine, mandible, pelvis) and three defined
by tissue appearance (brain tumor, multiple sclerosis lesions, breast lesions).
Each method trains on one modality and is evaluated in-domain and
out-of-domain. An ablation isolates the effect of texture preservation.
\textbf{Results.} PALETTE-Aug achieves the highest Dice on six of the eight
tasks and significantly outperforms every competing method overall. In the
ablation (six tasks), preserving texture raises out-of-domain Dice, pooled per
task, by $15\%$ and $16\%$ relative to noise fill on two appearance-defined
targets (brain tumor, multiple sclerosis lesions) and by at most $4\%$ on
interface-bounded ones (healthy brain: $-3\%$). Breast lesions, also
appearance-defined, show no Dice gain.
\textbf{Conclusion.} Texture preservation helps most where the target is
defined by tissue appearance rather than by an anatomical interface, though not
on every such target, and PALETTE-Aug generalizes best
overall across eight segmentation tasks.
\end{abstract}
