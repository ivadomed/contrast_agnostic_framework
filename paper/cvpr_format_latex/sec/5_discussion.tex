\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why anatomy predicts the largest effects.} Liver, spleen and kidney are
encapsulated organs with a clear physical boundary, and edge-following methods
have long segmented them well~\cite{lee2007gaclivers}. An
augmentation that scrambles their internal texture but keeps the boundary loses
little. Diffuse glioma has no such boundary: tumor cells infiltrate beyond both
the enhancing margin and the FLAIR abnormality~\cite{gliomamargins2025,flairectomy2022},
so the target must be inferred from appearance. MS lesions, though typically
well demarcated, are identified by abnormal signal within white matter, and
texture features separate them from normal-appearing white
matter~\cite{zhang2008mstexture}. This account interprets the
intervention of \cref{subsec:e-ablation}; it is not separate evidence for it.

\paragraph{Not every appearance-defined target gains.} Open-MS T1w, BraTS-GLI
T2w and FLAIR, and both Breast rows show no Dice gain. We have two hypotheses.
The effect differs sharply by training contrast within one task (BraTS-GLI:
$+13.3$ from T1c, $+7.2$ from T1n, about zero from T2w and FLAIR), and from T2w
real fill helps some labels and hurts others (\cref{par:per-label}), so
whether the training contrast's texture transfers may matter as much as the
target. And Breast pools four cohorts from different
trials, which may dilute a cohort-specific effect. On interface-bounded
tasks, where texture adds little relative to the starting Dice, most of the
gain arrives with the texture-free partition rungs (\cref{tab:ladder-full}),
that is, from the wide range of region-to-region contrasts PALETTE-Aug
samples.

\paragraph{Limitations.} We assign each target to a group by its anatomy, not
by a measured boundary statistic. Target type is confounded with difficulty:
every interface-bounded row starts above $63\%$ noise-fill Dice and every
appearance-defined row below $42\%$, so dividing by the starting Dice favors
the latter; as a share of the remaining error, CHAOS T2spir would rank first.
The group contrast is descriptive: with three ladder tasks per group, no test
of it can reach $p<0.05$. Open-MS has eight test patients and ON-Harmony eight
sessions of four subjects, so their single-setting effects can be consistent
without reaching significance, and
ON-Harmony's session-level tests are optimistic. Spine was run on a separate
pipeline and has no SRCSM arm. The cross-task test treats our eight tasks as
fixed; with the task as the unit, the advantage over SRCSM is not significant.
PALETTE-Aug targets the single-source setting and is complementary to
multi-source domain generalization, which assumes several labeled source
domains.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked when preserving real image texture matters for contrast-robust
segmentation. Its largest gains, relative to where the model starts, occur
where the target is defined by tissue appearance rather than by an anatomical
interface ($+15\%$ on brain tumor and $+16\%$ on MS lesions, pooled per task,
against at most $4\%$ on interface-bounded tasks), though not on every
appearance-defined target. PALETTE-Aug, which
preserves texture and needs no dense label map, is the best method overall
across eight tasks and sixteen datasets. The task-level account makes a
testable prediction for new tasks: a large relative gain from texture
preservation should not be expected where the target has an anatomical
interface.
