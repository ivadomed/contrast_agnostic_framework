\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why the effect follows the anatomy.} Liver, spleen and kidney are
encapsulated organs with a clear physical boundary, and edge-following methods
have long segmented them well~\cite{kavur2021chaos,lee2007gaclivers}. An
augmentation that scrambles their internal texture but keeps the boundary loses
little. Diffuse glioma has no such boundary: tumor cells infiltrate beyond both
the enhancing margin and the FLAIR abnormality~\cite{gliomamargins2025,flairectomy2022},
so the target must be inferred from appearance. MS lesions are likewise defined
by tissue signal~\cite{zhang2008mstexture}. This account interprets the
intervention of \cref{subsec:e-ablation}; it is not separate evidence for it.

\paragraph{Appearance-defined is necessary, not sufficient.} Open-MS T1w,
Brats-GLI T2w and FLAIR, and both Breast rows show no texture effect. We have
two untested hypotheses. The effect differs sharply by training contrast within
one task (Brats-GLI: $+13.3$ from T1c, $+7.2$ from T1n, about zero from T2w and
FLAIR), so how much discriminative texture the training contrast carries may
matter as much as the target. And Breast pools four cohorts from different
trials, which may dilute a cohort-specific effect. On interface tasks, where
texture adds little, PALETTE-Aug's gains likely come mostly from the wider range
of region-to-region contrasts, including sign inversions, that PALETTE-Aug
samples.

\paragraph{Limitations.} We assign each target to the interface or
no-interface group by its anatomy, not by a measured boundary statistic. We
state the texture effect relative to the noise-fill Dice; in absolute points,
CHAOS T2spir ($+5.1$) exceeds several appearance-defined settings.
Open-MS has eight test patients and ON-Harmony eight sessions of four subjects,
so their effects can be consistent without reaching significance, and
ON-Harmony's session-level tests are optimistic. Spine was run on a separate
pipeline and has no SRCSM arm. The cross-task test treats our eight tasks as
fixed; with the task as the unit, the advantage over SRCSM is not significant.
PALETTE-Aug targets the single-source setting and is complementary to
multi-source domain generalization, which assumes several labeled source
domains.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked when preserving real image texture matters for contrast-robust
segmentation. Its largest gains, relative to where the model starts, occur
where the target is defined by tissue appearance rather than by an anatomical
interface ($+44\%$ and $+18\%$ on brain tumor, $+39\%$ on MS lesions, at most
$+6\%$ on interface targets), though not on every appearance-defined target. PALETTE-Aug, which
preserves texture and needs no dense label map, is the best method overall
across eight tasks and sixteen datasets. The task-level account makes a
testable prediction for new tasks: a large relative gain from texture
preservation should not be expected where the target has an anatomical
interface.
