\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Aggregate margins are thin by design of the evaluation, not by
accident.} Every setting in \cref{sec:experiments} is single-source
cross-contrast, the hardest regime in which to demonstrate a gain: the
strongest available competitor is our own ablation
(\texttt{auglab\_default}), which already includes a strong general-purpose
augmentation stack. We view the consistency of the win -- eight out of
eight settings, spanning three anatomical regions and two modalities -- as
more informative than any single margin, and we report the near-ties
explicitly (BraTS, OpenMS T1w) rather than omitting them.

\paragraph{The causal ablation isolates a mechanism, not a full explanation.}
The noise-fill comparison in \cref{subsec:e-ablation} shows that texture
preservation, specifically, drives the advantage on texture-defined
structures; it does not by itself explain the smaller, more uniform gains
observed on boundary-defined structures, which likely stem from the wider
space of region-to-region contrast relationships (including the sign
inversions in \cref{eq:affine}) that no single real acquisition protocol
supplies. Disentangling these two effects further is left to future work.

\paragraph{SynthSeg-noEM's collapse warrants a caveat.} The gap between
SynthSeg-noEM and SynthSeg-EM in \cref{tab:main} is large enough that we
re-examined the SynthSeg-noEM configuration; we found no discrepancy from a
standard configuration, but as with any re-implemented baseline, we cannot
rule out that a different hyperparameter choice would narrow this specific
gap, and the paper's central comparisons do not depend on the size of that
gap.

\paragraph{Foreground segmentation is assumed, not solved.} $k$-means
partitioning in \cref{subsec:m-transform} operates on a label-free
foreground mask (e.g.\ an intensity threshold), not a learned or curated
one; on acquisitions where foreground/background separation itself is
difficult, the quality of this mask becomes a dependency we have not
stress-tested here.

\paragraph{Scope.} PALETTE targets the single-source setting: one labelled
training modality, no access to target-domain data of any kind. It is
input-level and architecture-agnostic, and we do not claim it subsumes
multi-source domain generalization methods that have access to several
labelled source domains; the two are complementary rather than competing
solutions to the same problem.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced PALETTE, a label-free, single-source contrast augmentation
that partitions the foreground of a scan by unsupervised $k$-means and
Voronoi tessellation and applies a signed affine remap of the real
intensities within each resulting sub-region, in contrast to label-generative
synthesis methods that require a dense anatomical label map and replace
real intensities with sampled noise. Across eight cross-contrast and
cross-modality segmentation settings spanning abdominal, brain, and brain
tumor imaging, a single nnU-Net configuration trained with PALETTE
significantly outperforms a no-augmentation baseline and the original
SynthSeg in every setting, and a re-implemented label-free SynthSeg variant
in seven of eight. A causal ablation that holds the partitioning scheme
fixed and only swaps real-intensity remapping for label-style noise fill
shows that texture preservation, not the shared partitioning scheme, drives
the advantage on texture-defined structures -- directly supporting the
premise that motivated the method: that contrast-robust segmentation does
not require discarding the real texture of the training image.
