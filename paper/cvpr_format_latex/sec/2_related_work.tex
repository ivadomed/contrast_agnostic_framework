\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Domain generalization for medical segmentation}
\label{subsec:dg}

Domain generalization (DG) targets unseen distributions without access to
target data, unlike unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), which assumes
unlabelled target images and solves an easier problem~\cite{shin2023sdcuda}.
Most medical DG methods are \emph{multi-source}, interpolating across several
labelled domains by meta-learning~\cite{li2018mldg}, feature
alignment~\cite{wang2020dofe}, or frequency-space
exchange~\cite{liu2021feddg}. Recent computer-vision entrants follow the same
recipe: MADGNet couples a multi-frequency architecture with multi-domain
training~\cite{nam2024madgnet}, and foundation models such as SynthFM pursue
modality-agnostic segmentation at scale~\cite{sengupta2025synthfm}, while
diffusion-based stylization normalizes appearance but relies on a generative
prior and on source diversity~\cite{bao2024structaware}. We instead work in the
\emph{single-source} regime --- one labelled dataset, no target data, no second
source --- which cannot exploit cross-domain interpolation and makes
architecture-coupled methods orthogonal: our mechanism acts at the input level
and leaves the segmentation network a fixed nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet}.

\subsection{Synthesis-based contrast-agnostic training}
\label{subsec:synth}

The dominant single-source strategy is label-generative domain randomization,
introduced by SynthSeg~\cite{billot2023synthseg}: intensities are sampled from a
per-label Gaussian mixture over an anatomical segmentation, yielding images of
arbitrary contrast that force contrast-invariance. This family carries two
structural costs. It is \emph{label-driven} --- a dense anatomical label map is
required per subject, and SIAM shows the dependence is on label \emph{quality},
needing a few high-grade manually curated templates~\cite{valabregue2026siam};
producing such maps is expensive in expert hours. It is also
\emph{texture-destroying}: per-label Gaussian sampling replaces real gradients,
partial-volume detail and unlabelled structure with piecewise noise, retaining
layout but not texture. The lineage has been specialized to white-matter
lesions~\cite{laso2024wmhsynthseg} and MS
lesions~\cite{caselles2026timelesseg}, and sits alongside training-free
per-scan generative fitting such as SAMSEG~\cite{cerri2021samseg}. We take
SynthSeg's label-free (EM) and original (noEM) variants as our primary baseline;
as the EM variant has no public code, a faithful re-implementation is itself a
contribution.

\subsection{Single-source intensity and appearance augmentation}
\label{subsec:aug}

A second line manufactures appearance diversity directly from the real image,
with no generative label model. General-purpose libraries stack intensity,
spatial and artifact transforms on an nnU-Net trainer; we build on one such GPU
framework~\cite{molinier2026auglab,isensee2021nnunet}, which also serves as our
augmentation-only ablation, and BigAug follows the same stacked-transform
philosophy~\cite{zhang2020bigaug}. Closer to our operator are random-filter
methods: GIN applies randomly re-initialized convolutions per forward pass to
produce label-free appearance variants in true 3D with no trained
parameters~\cite{ouyang2022gin}, RandConv establishes the principle for natural
images~\cite{xu2021randconv}, SLAug perturbs intensities per class using the
task mask~\cite{su2023slaug}, and diffusion variants add learned
generators~\cite{chen2024diffusionsdg}; a parallel frequency-domain line
perturbs the Fourier amplitude spectrum rather than the image
itself~\cite{zhao2024morestyle,li2024raffesdg,qiao2024ras}. These share our
label-free, single-source, architecture-agnostic stance --- GIN especially is a
direct peer --- but vary appearance globally, spatially or spectrally, whereas
our operator partitions the foreground into intensity classes and Voronoi
sub-regions and applies a signed affine remap of the \emph{real} intensities
within each. The closest peer is semantic-aware random
convolution~\cite{thaler2025semrandconv}, which also localizes the perturbation
region-wise in true 3D, but differs on both axes that define our operator: it
delineates regions from the \emph{ground-truth label map}, reintroducing the
task-label dependence we avoid, and applies a random convolution within each,
which alters local gradients, whereas we partition label-free by $k$-means and
Voronoi tessellation and apply an affine remap that leaves gradients intact.
Because the remap is affine and region-local, edges and texture survive while
contrast is randomized, preserving exactly the signal label-generative synthesis
discards, without any anatomical labels.

\subsection{The evaluation gap}
\label{subsec:gap}

We are not aware of prior work reporting our protocol: training on a
\emph{single real input modality} and testing on held-out modalities of the
\emph{same task and dataset}, across a benchmark spanning brain, abdominal and
tumour anatomy in both MRI and CT. Existing single-source evaluations are either
cross-dataset but same-modality --- e.g.\ FLAIR MS-lesion models across
sites~\cite{badea2024generalizable} on cohorts such as the public 3D-MR-MS
data~\cite{lesjak2018openms} --- or contrast-agnostic-by-synthesis, where
training data already span all contrasts and ``single-modality'' refers only to
the test input. The one-real-modality-in, held-out-modality-out axis is the
harder setting, and the one we target.
